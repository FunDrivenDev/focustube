# Mission

focustube filters the noise on YouTube, so you see only what you chose to see, and turns watching into learning.

YouTube is built to keep you watching: the next video, the sidebar, the shorts shelf. A viewer who opened it for a tech conference ends up on a Warhammer video an hour later. focustube starts from the other end: you name a theme, you pick the sources you trust about it, and the app shows you their videos and nothing else, in an organized feed without "next video" suggestions.

It then helps you decide where to spend your time. It reads the transcripts, summarizes them, pulls out what is worth keeping, finds the books, articles and people a video quotes, and keeps your own notes next to each video. What it learns about your preferences stays on your Mac.

## Principles

- **You choose, the app follows.** Nothing reaches the feed that you did not ask for, directly or by a preference the app learned and you can see and undo.
- **No attention traps.** No autoplay, no "next video", shorts off with one key.
- **Local first.** Preferences, history, notes and summaries live in a local database; an account is optional.
- **Agents are optional.** An agent such as Claude Code can do the analysis, but every step (tokens, rate limits, fetching) also works as plain code.
- **Open and fund-driven.** Built in the open by Fun Driven Dev.

## Workflow

1. Start with a [theme](features/themes.md).
2. Collect the [trusted sources](features/sources.md) about it.
3. Read the [feed](features/feed.md), and teach it with [per-video feedback](features/feedback.md).
4. [Analyze](features/analysis.md) a video to decide whether to watch it, and keep its [learnings](features/analysis.md#learnings), [references](features/references.md) and [notes](features/notes.md).

The [features](features/README.md) list every part in detail.
